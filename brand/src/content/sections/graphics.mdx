<Block label="Graphic categories" text="hzero's product mostly runs in the background, so there is little to screenshot. Choose deliberately which level a moment needs." action="Download charts" href="downloads/hzero-charts.zip">
  <Grid cols={3}>
    <Card eyebrow="Level 1" title="The idea">A conceptual visual that gives the gist in seconds: many parts becoming one coordinated system. Mostly grey, one cyan element. The level most often missing today; lead with it.</Card>
    <Card eyebrow="Level 2" title="How it works">The exact technical diagram: workloads, schedulers, QPUs, HPC nodes, operator objectives. For technical audiences and the second half of a deck.</Card>
    <Card eyebrow="Level 3" title="The product">Real interface: controls and results. Use as proof, after the idea is clear. Never lead with a dashboard.</Card>
  </Grid>
  <Quote by="Martin">Our product is not the visual dashboard. Our product is managing a complex system.</Quote>
</Block>

    <Card title="hzero is the only accent">Everything else in greys. hzero's layer is charcoal with its name in cyan, or the single deep cyan element on white.</Card>
    <Card title="Show two-way traffic">hzero asks every part how it is doing and decides from that. Two-way arrows, not a one-way pipe.</Card>
    <Card title="Equal partners">Workloads, quantum and HPC sit at the same level. Never draw one as subordinate to the others.</Card>
    <Card title="Lines, not pictures">1.5 px lines in mist or slate, flat fills, Inter labels. No 3D, no isometric stacks, no glowing qubits.</Card>
    <Card title="Less than you know">Leave out what the audience doesn't need for this slide; put it in the level-2 diagram instead.</Card>
    <Card title="Recognisable when copied">Colour and form alone should say hzero in someone else's deck. "Diagrams people steal."</Card>
  </Grid>
</Block>
