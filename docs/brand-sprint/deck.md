# hZero Brand Sprint: Deck Content

Two sessions of 2.5 hours. Day 1 (Discovery & Divergence): the founders give raw input. Overnight: the facilitator translates it into three visual directions. Day 2 (Convergence & Definition): the founders choose, as curators.

The slides are built in Figma on the `slides` page of the Hzero file: one 1920 × 1080 frame per slide, with the facilitator notes in a separate frame to the right of each slide. The notes frames are not part of the presentation.

**Participants:** the two hZero founders.

**Ground rule for every slide:** no design vocabulary on screen. Words like "kerning", "brand architecture" and "visual language" stay in the facilitator's head. On screen, everything is a question a founder can answer from their own experience.

## DAY 1 · DIVERGENCE & ALIGNMENT

### Slide 1 · Welcome & Ground Rules

**Title:** You bring the truth. I bring the craft.
**Subtitle:** hZero brand sprint · Day 1 of 2 · 2.5 hours

**Visual layout:** Split slide. Left 7 columns: the title, very large, and one sentence under it. Right 5 columns: three stacked rule cards, numbered, with square corners. Bottom strip: today's agenda as a thin timeline with four stops and the time per stop.

**On-slide copy:**
- Lead: Today we are not designing anything. We are collecting what only you know.
- Rule cards:
  1. **No wrong answers.** A strong, strange opinion is worth more than a safe one.
  2. **Say it, don't draw it.** You describe. I translate. Nobody here needs to be a designer.
  3. **Real over polished.** Stories from the lab, the server room and the sales call beat anything that sounds like a brochure.
- Timeline: Personality sliders · 40 min / Mood & feeling · 40 min / Raw truths · 50 min / Wrap-up · 20 min

**Facilitator script:**
- Open by naming the fear directly: "Most founders assume a brand sprint is a test of taste. It isn't. You are the only people in the room who know what hZero actually is. My job is to turn that into something people can see."
- Make the role split concrete: today they are witnesses, tomorrow they are judges. They will never be asked to pick a font from a list of fifty.
- Ask for one thing before starting: phones away, and permission to interrupt when an answer sounds like marketing copy. Agree a word for it, for example "brochure!".
- Warm-up (3 min): "Explain hZero to a smart 12-year-old in one sentence." Write the answers down word for word. These are often the best raw material of the whole day.

### Slide 2 · Brand Personality Sliders

**Title:** Where does hZero sit?
**Subtitle:** Four scales. Place your dot. Then defend it.

**Visual layout:** Four full-width slider rows, stacked. Each row has a left word, a thin track with 7 tick marks, and a right word. Beneath the tracks is a dot tray with one colour per founder: lime for founder 1, black for founder 2 (four dots each). A right-hand column (4 columns wide) holds the "unpack" questions. The facilitator moves the dots live on the canvas, or the founders drag them.

**On-slide copy:**
- Instruction: Each of you places one dot per scale, on your own, without talking. Then we compare.
- Scales:
  1. Traditional ←→ Disruptive
  2. Minimalist ←→ Maximalist
  3. Playful ←→ Authoritative
  4. Accessible ←→ Exclusive
- Unpack questions:
  - What would a customer have to see to believe your dot is right?
  - Who in your market sits at the opposite end, and why is that wrong for you?
  - Where were you two years ago on this scale? Where will you be in five?
  - If you moved your dot one step, what would we lose?

**Facilitator script:**
- Silent placement first (2 min per scale), so the most senior voice doesn't anchor the others.
- Translate each scale into hZero's world before they vote:
  - **Traditional ↔ Disruptive:** "Do you fit into the HPC centre's existing tools, or do you replace how they work?"
  - **Minimalist ↔ Maximalist:** "Does the operator see one clear number, or the whole system?"
  - **Playful ↔ Authoritative:** "Would you joke in front of a supercomputer centre's procurement board?"
  - **Accessible ↔ Exclusive:** "Should a research group with a small budget feel invited?"
- Where dots disagree by more than two ticks, stop and dig: that gap is a decision, not noise. Write the reasons on the canvas next to the dots.
- Don't average. Record the final agreed position plus any "dissent" note. Both go into the overnight synthesis.

### Slide 3 · Visual Mood & Emotional Benchmarking

**Title:** How should hZero feel in five years?
**Subtitle:** Pin five images. Talk about the feeling, not the colours.

**Visual layout:** Five large, empty image slots in a row (portrait 3:4), each with a dashed outline and a numbered caption field underneath. The slots are labelled with what to find, not what to show. A strip under the slots holds three "feeling" prompts. The facilitator brings a curated pool of about 60 printed or on-screen images (machines, materials, places, people, nature, objects); founders may also bring their own photos.

**On-slide copy:**
- Instruction: Choose five images from the pool, or from your own phone, that capture how hZero should feel when it has won. They don't need to show computers.
- Slot labels:
  1. **The feeling of the product working:** what does "it just runs" feel like?
  2. **A material or texture:** if hZero were made of something, what would it be?
  3. **A place:** where would hZero feel at home?
  4. **A person or a moment:** who is relieved, proud or calm because of you?
  5. **The wildcard:** an image you love and can't explain.
- Feeling prompts, one per image:
  - What do you feel first, in one word?
  - What would be wrong if this image were cleaner, glossier or more "techy"?
  - What sound would this image make?

**Facilitator script:**
- Pre-curate the pool so it avoids stock "quantum" imagery: no glowing blue circuits, no floating qubits, no handshakes. Include raw materials (machined aluminium, cable looms, concrete, paper with pencil notes), calm control rooms, and quiet human moments.
- Steer away from colour and type talk: "Forget the colours. If this image were a temperature, what would it be?"
- Ask the founders to name the image in their own words, for example "the 3am calm", and write that name under the slot. Those names become themes.
- Watch for repeated words across the five images. Circle them; they go straight into the overnight synthesis.

### Slide 4 · The "Anti-AI Slop" & Raw Truths Exercise

**Title:** What could only hZero say?
**Subtitle:** If a machine could have written it, it doesn't count.

**Visual layout:** Four container cards in a 2 × 2 grid, each with a title, two prompt questions and an empty answer area with lines. A narrow band on top shows the "slop test": a generic sentence crossed out next to an empty line for the hZero version. The facilitator types the answers live into the cards.

**On-slide copy:**
- Slop test: ~~"We empower organisations to unlock the full potential of next-generation computing."~~ Now say what you actually do, in words your engineers would use.
- Card 1 · **Unpopular opinions**
  - What does everyone in quantum say that you think is nonsense?
  - What will the industry be embarrassed about in five years?
- Card 2 · **Founder fingerprints**
  - What habit, obsession or background do you bring that nobody else in this market has?
  - What's the strangest thing on your desk, and why is it there?
- Card 3 · **Honest flaws**
  - What are you not good at yet, and what does that say about what you prioritise?
  - What would you never build, even if a customer paid for it?
- Card 4 · **Behind the scenes**
  - Describe the worst night you've had running a hybrid job. What happened?
  - What does your team's whiteboard look like on a Friday?

**Facilitator script:**
- Explain why this matters in one line: "Anything an algorithm can generate, your competitors can too. Specific, slightly uncomfortable truths are the only thing nobody can copy."
- Run the slop test together first: read the generic sentence aloud, then ask each founder to rewrite it. The first answers will still be generic; push twice: "More specific. Which customer? Which machine? What broke?"
- Record exact phrases, including swear words and in-jokes. Do not tidy them up. Mark the three lines that made the room laugh or go quiet; they are the strongest voice signals.
- If a founder freezes, switch to stories: "Tell me about the last time a customer thanked you." Stories surface truths faster than questions about values.

### Slide 5 · Wrap-up & Overnight Bridge

**Title:** Tonight I translate. Tomorrow you choose.
**Subtitle:** What happens between now and Day 2

**Visual layout:** Two columns with a single arrow between them. Left: "What you gave me today", four short summary lines filled in live. Right: "What you'll see tomorrow", three numbered direction placeholders (A, B, C) as empty cards. Bottom: Day 2 start time and one small ask.

**On-slide copy:**
- What you gave me today:
  1. Your positions on four personality scales
  2. Five images and the feelings behind them
  3. The raw truths only hZero can say
  4. The words you kept repeating
- What you'll see tomorrow:
  - **Three visual directions.** Each one has colours, type, imagery and a voice.
  - **Each direction is a real option.** None is a decoy.
  - **Your job:** react, compare, choose, combine.
- Overnight ask: If something comes to mind tonight, a photo, a sentence, a rival you hate, send it. Nothing is too small.

**Facilitator script:**
- Read back the strongest three quotes of the day, word for word. This proves they were heard and builds trust in the overnight work.
- Set expectations honestly: the directions will be rough, and that's deliberate. They are meant to be reacted to, not approved.
- Explain the rule for Day 2: "You don't have to like a direction to learn from it. 'I hate this' is a perfectly useful answer, as long as you tell me why."
- Close on time. Tired founders make conservative choices on Day 2.

## OVERNIGHT INTERLUDE (facilitator only)

### Slide 6 · The Synthesis Engine

**Title:** From raw input to three directions
**Subtitle:** Internal checklist · not shown to the founders

**Visual layout:** A four-lane pipeline, left to right: **Inputs → Patterns → Translation → Packages**. Each lane is a column of checklist items with checkboxes. Under the pipeline is a matrix with Direction A, B and C as rows and Color, Type, Imagery and Voice as columns, with an empty cell for each.

**On-slide copy:**
- Inputs:
  - [ ] Final slider positions and dissent notes
  - [ ] Five pinned images with founder names
  - [ ] Raw-truth quotes, with the top three starred
  - [ ] Repeated words list
- Patterns:
  - [ ] Cluster the quotes into 3–5 human themes
  - [ ] Note where the sliders conflict with the stories
  - [ ] List the "never" items: what hZero must never look or sound like
- Translation rules:
  - [ ] Each slider sets one visual dial: contrast, density, type weight, colour saturation
  - [ ] Each image sets one texture or imagery rule
  - [ ] Each starred quote becomes a voice example
- Packages, per direction:
  - [ ] Colour: 1 primary, 2 secondary, 1 accent, with hex codes
  - [ ] Type: one header and one body pairing, shown on a real hZero sentence
  - [ ] Imagery: 3 example images plus 3 rules
  - [ ] Voice: 3 adjectives plus a rewritten homepage line
- Quality gate:
  - [ ] Could a competitor use this direction unchanged? If yes, redo it.
  - [ ] Is every direction rooted in a founder quote or image?
  - [ ] Are the three directions really different from each other?

**Facilitator script (notes to self):**
- Spread the directions deliberately: one that pushes the sliders to their edge, one that sits on the agreed positions, and one that follows the strongest story even where it contradicts the sliders.
- Show every direction on the same three real artefacts, so the founders compare like with like: the homepage hero, a slide title, and a LinkedIn post.
- Label each direction with a human name drawn from their own words (for example "The 3am calm"), not a design term.
- Time box: 4 hours of making, 30 minutes of checking against the quality gate.

## DAY 2 · CONVERGENCE & DEFINITION

### Slide 7 · Welcome Back & Recap

**Title:** Here's what you told me
**Subtitle:** Day 2 of 2 · Today you decide

**Visual layout:** Top half: the four sliders from Day 1, now showing the agreed position as a single solid marker and any dissent as a hollow marker. Bottom half: four theme cards, each with a theme name, the founder quote it came from, and a pinned image thumbnail.

**On-slide copy:**
- Instruction: Is anything here wrong or missing? Correct me before we start choosing.
- Sliders: Traditional ←→ Disruptive · Minimalist ←→ Maximalist · Playful ←→ Authoritative · Accessible ←→ Exclusive, with the agreed positions marked.
- Theme cards, filled in overnight. Card format: **Theme name** · "Founder quote" · image.
- Footer: Today's role: curators. You choose, combine and reject. I build.

**Facilitator script:**
- Spend no more than 15 minutes here. The goal is agreement on the inputs, not a second debate.
- Invite corrections explicitly: "If I've misheard something, now is the cheapest moment to fix it."
- Reconfirm the role switch: "Yesterday you talked and I listened. Today I'll show you options, and your job is to be ruthless."

### Slide 8 · Three Visual Directions

**Title:** Three ways hZero could look and sound
**Subtitle:** React first. Explain second. Choose last.

**Visual layout:** Three equal columns, A, B and C, each a tall container card containing:
- a colour strip with 4 swatches and their hex codes
- a type specimen: a header and body pairing shown on the same hZero sentence
- three imagery rules
- three voice words

Under the columns is a voting row, one per column: "Love" and "Steal this" stickers, and an "Avoid" sticker.

**On-slide copy:**
- **A · Raw & Provocative:** "Quantum isn't magic. It's plumbing. We fix the plumbing."
  - Colour: Black #0A0A0A · White #FFFFFF · Lime #E2F28E (accent) · Grey #8D8D8D
  - Type: Selecta Black for headers + Inter Regular for body. Huge, tight headlines.
  - Imagery: real hardware close-ups, harsh flash, no stock photos, visible cables and labels.
  - Voice: blunt · funny · certain
- **B · Industrial & Precise:** "One operational layer across the system."
  - Colour: Light grey #E5E5E5 · Ink #0A0A0A · Olive #82816D · White #FFFFFF
  - Type: Selecta Regular for headers + Inter Regular for body. Grid-locked, generous space.
  - Imagery: diagrams, measured data, engineering drawings, pixel-precise charts.
  - Voice: exact · calm · expert
- **C · Warm & Humanistic:** "Behind every hybrid job is a team that wants to go home on time."
  - Colour: Warm paper #F6F5F2 · Sand #A38457 · Green #446455 · Ink #0A0A0A
  - Type: Selecta Light for headers + Inter Light for body. Softer, more air.
  - Imagery: people at work, hands, whiteboards, natural light, real offices.
  - Voice: generous · clear · reassuring
- Voting instruction: Each of you gets 3 "Love" dots, 3 "Steal this" dots and 1 "Avoid" dot. Spend them on whole directions or on single parts.

**Facilitator script:**
- Reveal all three at once, then stay quiet for 60 seconds. First reactions are the most honest.
- Ask for gut reactions before reasons: "Which one made you sit up? Which one made you wince?"
- Make clear that mixing is allowed: "You can take A's headline type with C's photography." The "Steal this" dots exist for exactly that.
- Avoid the trap of the "safe middle": if everyone gravitates to B because it feels familiar, ask "Which of these could a competitor already be using?"
- Close the round with a single sentence per founder: "If we launched tomorrow, I'd want ___ because ___."

### Slide 9 · Defining the Aesthetic Pillars

**Title:** The three rules everything must follow
**Subtitle:** Lock the pillars. Then set the dials.

**Visual layout:** Top: three large pillar cards, each with an empty title line and two empty rule lines, filled in live from the Day 2 votes. Bottom left: "contrast rules", a set of three pair-swatches showing allowed and forbidden combinations. Bottom right: "layout temperament", two sliders (Calm ←→ Energetic, Spacious ←→ Dense) with a marker to place.

**On-slide copy:**
- Instruction: From the parts you loved and stole, we write three pillars. Each pillar has a name and two rules a designer could check.
- Pillar template:
  - **Pillar name:** for example, "Show the machine"
  - **Always:** for example, real hardware and real data, never illustrations of "quantum"
  - **Never:** for example, glow effects, gradients that imitate light, stock futurism
- Contrast rules:
  - Text is always black on light, or white on black. No grey text on colour.
  - The accent colour is used for one thing per page.
  - Charts use only the agreed data colours.
- Layout temperament: place the marker on both scales.
  - Calm ←→ Energetic
  - Spacious ←→ Dense

**Facilitator script:**
- Draft the pillar names live, using the founders' own words from Days 1 and 2. Offer two options per pillar and let them pick; don't ask them to write from scratch.
- Test each rule with a yes/no question: "If an agency sends us a design with a glowing qubit, does this rule let us reject it?" If the answer isn't an obvious yes, the rule is too vague.
- Keep it to three pillars. A fourth pillar is usually a sign that the team hasn't made a choice yet.

### Slide 10 · Brand Voice & Un-AI-able Tone

**Title:** How hZero sounds when it's being itself
**Subtitle:** Three words, clear examples, and a test against sameness.

**Visual layout:** Top: three large adjective cards. Middle: a two-column "We are / We are not" table with four rows. Bottom: the "human edge" checklist, and a single before/after sentence pair.

**On-slide copy:**
- Three voice words, chosen from the Day 1 quotes. Example set: **exact · candid · warm**
- We are / We are not:

  | We are | We are not |
  |---|---|
  | Exact: "Cuts queue time by 40% on mixed workloads." | Vague: "Unlocks unprecedented performance." |
  | Candid: "Quantum hardware is still noisy. We plan for it." | Hype: "The quantum revolution is here." |
  | Warm: "Your operators shouldn't need a PhD to schedule a job." | Cold: "Leveraging middleware for workload orchestration." |
  | Specific: "Built with HPC centres in Germany and the EU." | Generic: "Trusted by leading organisations worldwide." |

- Human-edge checklist, for every piece of copy:
  - Does it name a real thing: a machine, a number, a person, a place?
  - Would an engineer on our team say it out loud without cringing?
  - Could a competitor publish it unchanged? If yes, rewrite it.
  - Is there one sentence that could only come from us?
- Before / after:
  - Before: "hZero empowers organisations to harness hybrid computing."
  - After: "hZero makes quantum and classical computers work as one system, so your team stops babysitting jobs at 3am."

**Facilitator script:**
- Pick the three words by vote from a shortlist of about eight, pulled from Day 1 quotes. Don't accept words that every company uses, like "innovative" or "trusted".
- Read the "We are not" column aloud in an exaggerated voice; laughter makes the difference stick.
- Give the founders the human-edge checklist as their tool for reviewing any copy, including copy written with AI tools: AI drafts are fine, but nothing ships until it passes the four questions.
- Have each founder rewrite one real sentence from their current website or pitch deck using the checklist, live.

### Slide 11 · The Minimum Viable Brand Kit & Next Steps

**Title:** Your brand kit, version 1
**Subtitle:** Everything a designer, developer or agency needs to start on Monday

**Visual layout:** A summary matrix, four rows by two columns. The left column holds the category and the right column holds the decision, with swatches and specimens inline. Rows: Colour, Typography, Imagery, Voice. A timeline strip at the bottom shows four milestones with owners and dates.

**On-slide copy:**
- **Colour:** primary, secondary and accent with hex codes, plus the one contrast rule. Filled in from the chosen direction, for example Ink #0A0A0A · White #FFFFFF · Olive #82816D · Lime #E2F28E (accent, one use per page).
- **Typography:** header and body pairing with sizes. For example, Selecta for titles, Inter for body text; no monospace anywhere.
- **Imagery:** three rules and three "never" examples.
- **Voice:** the three words plus the four-question human-edge checklist.
- Next steps:
  1. **Week 1:** The facilitator delivers the brand kit as a one-page PDF plus Figma library. Owner: facilitator.
  2. **Week 2:** Apply it to the website hero and the pitch deck title slides. Owner: facilitator + founders.
  3. **Week 3:** Review it in real use and adjust one thing. Owner: founders.
  4. **Week 4:** Buy the type licences, then roll out to LinkedIn, the email signature and the conference booth. Owner: founders.
- Closing line: A brand isn't finished. It's practised. This kit is version 1.

**Facilitator script:**
- Fill in the matrix live from the decisions on slides 8–10. Nothing on this slide should be new.
- Assign an owner and a date to every milestone before anyone leaves the room.
- Name the one licence issue openly: the Selecta font is currently a trial; it must be bought before the brand goes live.
- End with a round: "What will you do differently on Monday because of these two days?"
